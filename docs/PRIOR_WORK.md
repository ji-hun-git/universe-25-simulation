# Earlier Universe 25 research

What came before this repo, around the one colony it is calibrated to:
- Calhoun's own programme;
- the rodent studies that followed it;
- the human-crowding research it set off;
- the histories and critiques;
- the models.

Each entry says what was done, what was found, and what it means here. The record's own numbers
(Calhoun 1971 and 1973, Marsden 1972) are in `docs/RESEARCH_DIGEST.md` part A. This page is the
map around them.

**How it was compiled.** A literature check on 2026-11-17. Primary sources were read where
they could be reached. Otherwise the citation was checked in Crossref, PubMed or Europe PMC,
and the finding taken from a reliable secondary account.

**Confidence tags:**
- **high:** the primary source was read;
- **medium:** an abstract or a reliable secondary account;
- **low:** a title or a single secondary mention.

## The short version

- **The collapse was seen once.** Universe 25 died out (nearly) in one colony, reported by its
  author. No independent study has repeated the collapse.
- **The nearest attempts went the other way.**
  - Kessler's crowded mice stabilised, and re-paired mice bred again.
  - Moving or thinning Petrusewicz's confined populations restarted their growth.
  - Terman's deermice recovered once removed from the crowd.
  - Hammock's one thinned group recovered in part.
  - Only Marsden's transfers, from Universe 25 itself, support irreversibility.
- **Rodent work supports a density brake on breeding** (Christian, Southwick, Lloyd and
  Christian). That brake slows a colony; on its own it does not end one.
- **In people, density as such did little.**
  - It mattered little once household crowding and income were taken apart (Galle et al.,
    Stokols, Freedman, Kirmeyer).
  - Household crowding goes with withdrawal more than with violence (Gove et al., Regoeczi).
  - Crowded monkeys cope by avoidance and appeasement (Judge and de Waal).
- **Historians show the "behavioural sink" outran Calhoun's own programme.** In the popular
  story it eclipsed his later work on designing environments for cooperation (Ramsden and
  Adams).
- **Models since 2025 reproduce the curve's shape**, by hand-set phases, a curve fit or
  parameter sweeps. None of them instruments play.

## 1. Calhoun's programme

- **Towson (1947–49).** On the Johns Hopkins rodent-ecology project, Calhoun put five pregnant
  wild rats into a quarter-acre pen behind his house.
  - Over about 27 months the colony levelled at about 150 and never passed 200. He reckoned
    the pen could hold about 5,000.
  - This is where his idea comes from: social structure, not food, sets a population's
    ceiling.
  - Sources: Ramsden and Adams 2009; the 1963 monograph per catalogue records. *medium*
- **The NIMH rat pens (1958–62).** Four pens linked by ramps, in a barn near Rockville,
  Maryland.
  - Dominant males held the end pens, which had one entrance each; the rest packed into the
    middle pens.
  - The result was violence, hypersexuality, withdrawal and failed mothering. Calhoun named it
    the "behavioral sink" (*Scientific American* 206, 1962).
  - Infant mortality reached 96 % in the most disordered groups, against 80 % for comparable
    animals in a series without a sink.
  - The sink was partly a product of how the pens were built. Much of Universe 25's vocabulary
    comes from these rats. *high (the article) / medium (the rates, via Ramsden and Adams)*
- **The mouse universes (1968–73).**
  - The digest's part A has the numbers: Universe 25 and its four smaller siblings, which
    plateaued.
  - "Death squared" (1973) is the paper of a talk at the Royal Society of Medicine. Dugatkin
    dates the talk to 22 June 1972; Ramsden and Adams give 1971.
  - "From mice to men" (a 1973 lecture) carries the analogy to people. *high*
- **Later work (1970s–1986).** Calhoun turned to designed universes meant to raise
  cooperative, "creative" rodents that could live at higher density. He used conditioning and
  controlled access to food, nests and partners.
  - His writing from this period is mostly conceptual (1973, 1977, 1983).
  - NIMH ended his contract in 1983. He left in 1986 and died in 1995.
  - A single book review says that in Universe 34 mice learned to cooperate for resources
    and births stabilised. *low: no primary data found*

## 2. Rodent follow-ups, replications and non-replications

- **Christian (1950–65; with Davis and Lloyd).** Rising density enlarged the adrenals and
  suppressed reproduction: a hormonal brake that regulates the population. It is a candidate
  mechanism for Universe 25's breeding stop. *medium*
- **Southwick (1955).** Confined house mice with unlimited food stopped growing as litters
  died, which he linked to fighting near the nests. *medium (titles and a 1970 secondary
  account)*
- **Crowcroft and Rowe (1957, 1958); Crowcroft (1966, *Mice All Over*).** Colony growth to a
  plateau, and dispersal's effect on female fecundity. *low: titles only*
- **Petrusewicz (1957, 1963).** Moving a whole confined population, or adding or removing a
  few animals, restarted growth.
  - 1957: growth resumed after 43 of 54 transfers.
  - 1963: in 65 % of 264 runs, against 32 % of undisturbed controls.
  - The plateau behaves like a social state, not like lasting damage. *medium*
- **Kessler (1966).** The closest outside attempt.
  - Two dense populations grew to about 1,000 and 800 in eight to nine months, then
    stabilised.
  - The mice withdrew rather than fought, and mice re-paired in cages bred again. *high*
- **Terman (1968, 1973).** Prairie deermice stopped breeding at their plateau, but animals
  taken out recovered. *medium*
- **Lloyd and Christian (1969).** Breeding was limited through female rank, embryo
  resorption, inhibited oestrus and infant deaths. *medium*
- **Hammock (1971).** Swiss Webster mice in three groups of 16 for 6.5 months.
  - The crowded groups weaned no pups.
  - One group was thinned. It weaned 41 % of its next litter and 30 % of the one after, then
    none.
  - The uncrowded control also stopped weaning once it filled its apparatus. *high*
- **Marsden (1972).** Mice transferred out of Universe 25 into fresh pens largely failed to
  breed. It is the only support for irreversibility, and it comes from the same colony.
  *high*
- **Moore (1999, *Primates*).** Revisits Calhoun's pens from behavioural ecology, through pen
  design and territorial males. *low: known through Gwern*

## 3. Human crowding

- **Galle, Gove and McPherson (1972, *Science*).** In Chicago's community areas, persons per
  room carried most of the association with mortality, fertility, welfare and delinquency.
  Area density was weak. *medium: secondary accounts of a scanned text*
- **Stokols (1972).** Physical density and the experience of crowding are different things.
  Most later work keeps them apart. *medium*
- **Freedman (1975, *Crowding and Behavior*).** Experiments with people found no general harm
  from density. *medium*
- **Reviews.**
  - Fischer, Baldassare and Ofshe (1975) describe a research bandwagon pulled by Calhoun's rats
    and weak human evidence.
  - Kirmeyer (1978) finds area density has little effect once income is accounted for.
  - Choldin (1978) is a critical review. *high / medium*
- **Gove, Hughes and Galle (1979); Regoeczi (2008).** Household crowding goes with worse
  mental health. In Regoeczi's Toronto survey, crowded women were more depressed, and crowded
  men withdrew rather than turned aggressive. The withdrawal echoes Kessler more than
  Calhoun's violence. *medium / high*
- **Primates.** Rhesus monkeys crowded into a fifth of their space showed no rise in severe
  aggression, only more avoidance and appeasement (Judge and de Waal 1993; de Waal, Aureli and
  Judge 2000). *high*

## 4. Histories and critiques

- **Ramsden and Adams (2009, *J Social History*).**
  - The popular "sink" story eclipsed Calhoun's hopeful programme of design and hurt his
    standing.
  - They trace how the term spread into planning and the human sciences.
  - Ramsden's other papers follow it into those sciences (*Isis* 2011), back to the rat-control
    origins at Johns Hopkins (*History of the Human Sciences* 2012), and into planning debates
    (Adams and Ramsden, *Comparative Studies in Society and History* 2011). *high*
- **Gwern ("Does mouse utopia exist?", 2019–21).** The Universe 25 result is thinly
  documented, and inbreeding, mutational load or disease could explain it as well. *high*
- **Books (2024).** Adams and Ramsden's *Rat City* and Dugatkin's *Dr. Calhoun's Mousery* are
  archival histories. Day (2025, *London Review of Books*) reviews both. *medium / high*

## 5. Models of Universe 25

- **Mantzaris (2025, RatUtopia; arXiv:2508.08587).**
  - The main model is population-level, in monthly steps. An agent-based version is only in an
    appendix.
  - The phase timings and the schedule of uncertainty about rank are set by hand. The fit is
    judged by eye, with no error figure.
  - The paper calls the animals rats. *high*
- **Roman (2025, *LNCS* 16090).** A maximum-entropy fit of the curve's shape, with no
  mechanism. *medium*
- **Gwizdałła and Duś (2026, *Entropy* 28(2):169).** This is the README's earlier "Mouse
  utopia model".
  - An agent model in one-day steps, 100 runs per parameter set. Some runs come within about
    10 % of the targets, and the authors read Calhoun's outcome as one draw among many.
  - They fit 640 on day 315, not the record's 620. Their GitHub repository holds a data
    archive and no code. *high*
- **Carucci, Bertolotti et al. (2026, LIUC).**
  - An equation model and an agent model of a "behavioral sink", calibrated on Universe 25 and
    on the St. Matthew Island reindeer.
  - A sweep of 50,000 runs finds that mortality and baseline fertility dominate, with three
    regimes: immediate decline, delayed persistence, and overshoot then collapse.
  - *high (the sweep paper) / medium (the model, an abstract)*
- No NetLogo or system-dynamics model with a citable source was found.
- **This repo against them.**
  - The models above fit the curve.
  - This repo is calibrated to it and checked out of sample: the Tier 1 predictive check in
    NOTES 2026-11-09.
  - It uses the curve as a substrate for one further question, play without a play reward.
    None of the others instruments play.

## 6. Cultural reach

Ramsden and Adams (2009) trace the chain:
- Edward Hall's *The Hidden Dimension* (1966) took up the sink;
- Tom Wolfe's 1968 essay "O Rotten Gotham" applied it to New York;
- Lewis Mumford (1968) cited the rat experiments;
- a 1970 underground comic was subtitled *Tales from the Behavioral Sink*;
- *Mrs. Frisby and the Rats of NIMH* (1971) was written by Robert Conly (as Robert C.
  O'Brien), who Calhoun believed had visited his laboratory.

The 2010s internet revival left no citable source beyond a few articles and videos.

## What this repo takes from them

- **The targets** come from Calhoun 1973 and Marsden 1972 alone (digest part A).
- **Irreversibility is an assumption.** It is fitted to one colony and named as such
  (NOTES "Phase 2m"; digest A7).
  - Kessler, Petrusewicz, Terman and Hammock argue the other way.
  - A model that collapses and stays collapsed matches Universe 25, not rodent populations in
    general.
- **Withdrawal comes before violence** in the follow-ups (Kessler, Regoeczi, the primates).
  The model's withdrawn role is the analogue of Calhoun's "beautiful ones", not a
  measurement of them.
- **The confounds stay listed** (digest A7):
  - the pen design that permitted a sink;
  - inbred founders;
  - unsterilised bedding;
  - the mid-run reconfiguration;
  - about 150 animals removed.
- **No model of Universe 25 instruments play.** That gap is this repo's question.

## Corrections made to `docs/RESEARCH_DIGEST.md` on 2026-11-17

1. **"Young 1972"** is not a publication we could find. It is most likely J. Z. Young, who
   chaired Calhoun's Royal Society of Medicine talk and questioned the step from mice to men
   (Ramsden and Adams 2009).
2. **Hammock 1971.** Only one group was thinned, and the uncrowded control also stopped
   weaning once it filled its apparatus.
3. **Petrusewicz.** The 65 % is against 32 % of controls; the 1957 paper adds 43 of 54
   transfers.
4. **Calhoun 1962.** The 96 % and 80 % infant mortalities are the most disordered groups and
   comparable animals in a series without a sink. They are not pen-level rates.
5. **Citations.** DOIs and the correct titles are now in part E.
6. **The README's models.** The "Mouse utopia model" is Gwizdałła and Duś 2026; its repository
   has no code. RatUtopia's agent model is an appendix.

**Where sources disagree:**
- the Towson run: 27 or 28 months, or "two years";
- the year Calhoun joined NIMH: 1954 or 1956;
- the year of the Royal Society of Medicine talk: 1971 or 1972;
- the *Scientific American* volume, which some histories misprint as 306 (it is 206).

  - Universe 33: nothing found.
  - Universe 34: one book review only.
  - Ramsden and Adams mention a "Universe 133".
